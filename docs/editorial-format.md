# B(ornot)2B: international editorial format

Release approved by the owner on October 31, 2026. English is the default edition at https://bornot2b.com/; Russian remains under /ru/.

## Editorial promise

**B(ornot)2B — How business gets done.**

A weekly field guide to how companies buy, sell and work together. For suppliers, buyers, distributors, commercial managers and operators. The editorial unit is a commercial mechanism: a commitment, route to purchase, incentive, handoff or allocation of risk that the reader can recognize in their own work.

International means varied industries and countries, clear local context and specific operating lessons. It does not mean turning every announcement into a global trend. Manufacturing, distribution, logistics and trade remain as important as software and AI.

## Reading structure

1. **The Big Read** — a documented deal or operating change, with its terms and unknowns. One cover story with a unique illustration.
2. **The Brief** — up to seven short dispatches. Fewer when the evidence is weak. Dates refer to original publication; effective dates are separate.
3. **Inside the Workflow** — a second feature only when the evidence supports it. In issue 01, a cross-source analysis of separate announcements, not a fabricated combined alliance.
4. **Routes to Market** — sales, procurement access, distributors and partners. Deal Terms is a rotating lens within this section.
5. **Deal Economics** — what is bought, paid, financed and committed; who carries the risk.
6. **Operations** — a grounded logistics, manufacturing or procurement case. Buyer’s Desk, The Person in the Process and Model to Study are optional lenses.
7. **Reality Check** — evidence that tests a headline. After the Announcement, The Number and archival failure cases appear when useful.
8. **Books in Practice** — an editorial choice outside the news window: ideas from the actual book, short attributed quotations, original application scenarios and limits. No scores.
9. **The Monday Move** — one bounded team conversation or action. Avoid a generic list of productivity advice.

Tool Watch belongs beside the relevant process, rather than repeating the same announcement as a separate story. Not every lens must appear every week; the issue follows the strength of the material.

## Language and presentation

Use clear US English, concrete verbs and industry terms explained on first use. Currency is explicit (US$); dates use spelled-out months; metric units remain where the source uses them. Explain procurement institutions without treating their rules as universal. Avoid translated Russian idioms, invented interviews, synthetic expertise or claims of human verification that did not occur.

Retain the bee identity, system fonts, bright palettes and rounded layouts. White cartoon characters act out each story, with no frame around the illustration itself. Company marks provide editorial context, never an implied endorsement. Motion is optional and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Evidence and release workflow

1. Collect primary sources and record original publication date, event date, country, claim and limits.
2. Select the commercial question and check duplication with prior issues.
3. Write source facts separately from the magazine’s interpretation; disclose unavailable terms, predictions and testing status.
4. Check every number, denominator, currency basis and comparison period. No causal claim from a vendor case or observational study alone.
5. Edit English for terminology, rhythm and actual reader usefulness. A strong English-language human editorial review remains useful before a broad launch; none is claimed to have occurred here.
6. Verify links, images, language switch, canonical URLs, language alternates, mobile layout and motion controls.
7. The owner approves the concrete draft. Only then merge and manually run publication. A push or PR does not publish.
8. After release, check the live issue and keep a visible correction note for substantive changes to published material.
