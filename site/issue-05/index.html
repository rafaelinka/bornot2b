The Week in Deals

November 21–27, 2026

Manufacturing, distribution, logistics, and equipment. An agreement announcement, a booked order, and a completed delivery are different stages; each brief keeps that distinction visible.

01 / China / France · November 24, 2026

A bottle designed for the Chinese shelf

JD.com says it is working with Château Lascombes on an exclusive limited edition for China in 2027. The November 24 announcement followed a November 20 gathering of international partners; the signing date is unspecified. Direct sourcing is paired with bonded warehouses and temperature-controlled logistics: the claimed two-to-three-day delivery starts when the consumer orders. Our reading: the channel helps shape the assortment as well as move it. Volumes, pricing, the scope of exclusivity, and who pays for adaptation remain undisclosed.

Primary source
  1. JD.com, 24 November 2026

02 / China / Poland / European markets · November 23, 2026

One hub, three appliance brands

JoyLogistics announced the opening of a 37,000-square-meter distribution hub west of Warsaw for Haier, Candy, and Hoover products. It handles warehousing, inventory management, transportation, and customs clearance; Haier Europe describes it as the first phase of a redesigned European network. For an appliance supplier, the useful question is product availability across markets, beyond the warehouse footprint. The operator reports the opening and includes the customer's perspective, but publishes no utilization, savings, or measured delivery-time improvement.

Primary source
  1. JoyLogistics / Haier Europe, 23 November 2026

03 / Pakistan / international buyers · November 27, 2026

5,800 meetings do not equal $900 million in sales

In an Arab News interview, a FoodAg organizer reported $135.18 million in export contracts and $31.83 million in memorandums. Another $900 million is exhibitors' expected business following more than 5,800 B2B meetings, rather than realized revenue. He also described buyer visits to production facilities to assess capacity, quality, and food safety. The interview took place November 26 and was published November 27; we have not inspected the contracts or verified shipments. The useful exhibition scorecard separates meetings, commitments, and fulfilled orders.

Primary source
  1. Arab News: interview with TDAP agriculture director general Athar Khokhar

04 / United Kingdom · November 25, 2026

Pet food grows; channel margin tells another story

Pets at Home reported food sales of £445.9 million for the 28 weeks ended October 8, up 4.4% from £426.9 million. Meanwhile, gross margin for the entire retail segment declined from 44.1% to 43.7%; food margin is not disclosed separately. The retailer links trading momentum to its range reset and notes the continuing effect of earlier price investments. For manufacturers, category demand and channel economics deserve separate attention: higher retailer sales do not establish better terms for any particular supplier.

Primary source
  1. Pets at Home FY27 interim results, 25 November 2026, pp. 7–8 and 26

A repeat order with a 2028 delivery date

Mycronic received a repeat order from an existing Asian customer for an SLX laser mask writer, valued at $5–7 million. Delivery is scheduled for the second quarter of 2028. For industrial sellers, it illustrates the value of returning to an established customer platform and the long horizon involved in capacity planning. Mycronic attributes the choice to confidence in its equipment, but does not name the buyer or disclose the deposit, cancellation terms, or exact price.

Signed, with the proof still to come

Sensys Gatso's Canadian subsidiary signed a contract worth up to CAD12.2 million for traffic cameras, installation, and maintenance. Following a competitive request for proposals, the initial three-year term includes three optional three-year extensions. The contract remains subject to a successful proof of concept. Suppliers should notice the remaining technical gate even after selection. The announcement does not identify the buyer, test criteria, test costs, or guaranteed volume.

07 / United Kingdom / buyer location undisclosed · November 23, 2026

The first purchase order comes before the big contract

EnSilica is starting work on a navigation chip under a customer purchase order while a broader design-and-supply contract is finalized. Heads of terms are signed, but the company explicitly says the full contract is not certain. It estimates supply revenue of more than $80 million over ten years from 2030, excluding development fees. The useful commercial step is the initial PO that lets work begin before the entire program is contracted. That PO's value and the final terms remain undisclosed.

08 / Australia / United States / United Kingdom · November 26, 2026

Two big numbers, two different commitments

Titomic disclosed a subcontract for its US subsidiary with a $250 million ceiling through 2031; the framework permits separately funded task orders whose actual value was not disclosed. Babcock's £8.7 billion agreement with the UK Ministry of Defence contains £8 billion of contract backlog and a £0.7 billion framework, as the footnote explains. Babcock's five-year term starts December 1; a possible fifteen-year aggregate term is not guaranteed. Our reading: the word “contract” can hide very different levels of committed future work. Assessing a supplier requires funded obligations, timing, and cancellation provisions beyond the headline number.

At Fosun Diagnostics' request, Virax added India as the seventh market in their supply framework. Freight rates split by route

Freightos' weekly update shows Asia–US West Coast ocean rates down 6%, while East Coast rates were flat. Asia–Northern Europe fell 1%, but Asia–Mediterranean rose 20%. The page was published November 26, despite the November 25 update date in its title. For negotiations, that divergence supports checking the actual lane rather than invoking generally “cheaper freight.” These are the index provider's figures, not our recalculation of underlying transactions; individual quotes and surcharges can differ.

13 / Canada / United States · November 23, 2026

From a $7 million engagement to $75 million in capacity

Stantec announced a ten-year City of San Diego engineering and construction-services contract covering 11 dams, with $75 million in contract capacity. It expands dam-improvement services begun three years earlier under a $7 million capacity contract. For professional-services sellers, this illustrates expansion within an existing public-sector account, although the announcement does not document the city's selection reasoning. Capacity is not completed work, and the separate $3 billion estimate for the city's broader rehabilitation program is not a Stantec order.

14 / Germany / Europe · November 24, 2026

Express shipping gains weight

DHL Express Europe says daily shipment weight in Heavy Weight Express increased 8% over the past six months. It attributes demand to urgent equipment and component shipments for manufacturing, healthcare, and energy. The practical shipper question is when faster transport of a heavy part costs less than the downtime it avoids. Absolute weight and a detailed comparison base were not provided: more kilograms do not establish more customers or market-wide growth, and we have not tested the service's economics.

What supply-chain visibility cannot buy

Robert Handfield and Tom Linton describe a supply chain as a living network. We test that framework against response times, working capital, data quality, backup suppliers, and engineering changes—with original scenarios and a recalculation of an error in the book.

Take It to Work on Monday

01

Compare supply rights alongside price

Before comparing batch prices, record the committed date, readiness fee, binding forecast quantities, and order-change rights. Check whether an advance is credited against purchases so the same amount is not counted twice. Paying for a reservation does not establish its return.

Read the analysis ↗

02

Check the shared window, not total weeks saved

Match the availability intervals of required resources to the duration of the shared operation. Establish who can authorize irreversible preparation and what happens after a schedule change. Earlier readiness at one contractor may add resilience without advancing the project.

Read the analysis ↗

03

Keep the original request and first promise

Show original request coverage, performance against the first confirmation, and subsequent changes together. Otherwise, confirming less can improve the service score. Assess recovery through repeat purchases, alongside the first shipment that refills an empty channel.

Read the analysis ↗

04

Connect the signal to a feasible action

For one actual order, identify the last useful moment to change course and the time needed to verify, approve, and execute that change. Name the decision owner and an available alternative. A faster screen refresh cannot supply a missing resource or the right to revise a commitment.
