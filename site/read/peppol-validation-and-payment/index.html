Peppol: Paid is not a bank statement

November's document update reveals the boundary between a technically valid invoice, completed commercial performance, and cash.

In This Analysis
  1. What changed
  2. What our limited test showed
  3. Four facts that should remain separate
  4. One invoice, a credit note, and a bank entry

Review status: a limited test of two identifier rules was completed. On November 28 we executed expressions from the official November Schematron on five synthetic XML examples. This tests selected rules, not complete Peppol invoice conformance. We did not test an exchange provider, ERP, Invoice Response processing, or bank payments.

An invoice can be technically correct and commercially disputed. It can enter processing while the goods are still being inspected. Document processing can also finish without a new transfer—for example, with a credit note. Finance needs to distinguish those events. If an interface combines them into one green checkmark, automation creates false confidence.

What changed

November's BIS 3.1.0 was published on November 23. In Billing, several identifier checks move from warning to fatal after a transition period. An error that previously accompanied a document as a warning can now stop validation. This warrants checking master data and rejection handling, rather than declaring all historical invoices invalid.

The other procurement-document notes clarify Invoice Response, including self-billing: when the buyer issues the document, the seller sends the response. They also explain Paid for credit notes: it signals that document processing is finished and no further action is expected, rather than serving as remittance advice. Do not automatically interpret that label as a new cash receipt.

Our repeat check on November 28, 2026 found the same mandatory-use date on both official November release pages, for Billing and the other BIS documents: February 22, 2027. We use those primary pages for planning; the earlier discrepancy with indexed copies is not an alternative deadline. Agree the operational transition for your provider and rule set in advance. This is a specification-use date, not a universal legal deadline for every company and country.

What our limited test showed

We extracted two unmodified expressions from the official 3.1.0 file: R040 checks the format and check digit of a 13-digit GS1 identifier, GLN13; R054 checks the eight-digit Dutch Chamber of Commerce KVK format. We executed them with SaxonC-HE 13.1 and the upstream GLN function. The five XML files contain synthetic identifiers and are not complete invoices. We evaluated ten assertions in total, two per file.

Synthetic exampleChangeObserved result
Control InvoiceGLN 1234567890128, KVK 12345678Both selected rules passed
Wrong check digitChanged the final GLN digit from 8 to 9R040 failed
Overlong GLNAdded a leading 0, making 14 digitsThe checksum passed, but R040 failed its format requirement

The useful finding is that a valid checksum alone does not protect against the wrong identifier length. Master-data checks need both. Passing these two rules establishes neither registration nor correctness of the remaining document, delivery, or payment. This experiment did not process Paid; we checked that status's meaning against the specification.

Four facts that should remain separate

EventWhat it establishesWhat it does not prove
A document passes validationCompliance with the tested technical rulesAcceptance of goods or agreement with the amount
An invoice response arrivesA reported status for a particular documentA matching bank transaction
Paid on a credit noteCompletion in the specified Invoice Response senseA necessarily separate cash receipt
A bank entry existsA recorded movement of moneyCorrect allocation across every invoice

Our interpretation: finance needs separate evidence for the document, performance, and cash. Even a bank entry requires reconciliation to an obligation. One transfer can settle several invoices, while a credit note can reduce the amount due. These examples explain the risk of collapsing statuses; they do not describe your particular system.

One invoice, a credit note, and a bank entry

Consider an editorial example, unrelated to a real company and not executed in an ERP. A supplier invoices 1,000 currency units. The parties agree a return worth 200 and issue a credit note. Its Invoice Response arrives as Paid, meaning document processing is complete. A bank transfer of 800 then arrives.

StepAmount still due in this exampleEvidence
Invoice issued1,000Claim against the buyer
Credit note agreed and posted800The claim is reduced by 200
Incoming 800 reconciled to the invoice0A bank entry and reconciliation

If the system turns Paid on the credit note into another 200 of cash, it invents a receipt. If it closes the original 1,000 invoice solely on the credit note’s status, before the 800 arrives, it hides the unpaid balance. Before updating, trace precisely this case: the original invoice, the related 200 document, the remaining 800, and its settlement. The integration owner should show which field changes after the external status; accounting should confirm the meaning of that change. For a bad identifier, correct both the document and the authoritative master record, or the error will return.
