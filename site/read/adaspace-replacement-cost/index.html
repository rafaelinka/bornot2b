Reality Check

Who pays for a replacement after a failed launch?

An archival Adaspace case shows how a failed subcontract can leave the integrator with manufacturing losses and a continuing promise to its own customer.

Adaspace · China · November 5 document / January 2026 event · 3 min read

Launch services and replacement manufacturing create different costs. A conceptual illustration.

A January event in a November document

Adaspace’s November 5 prospectus describes a previously disclosed January launch in which two uninsured satellites with a combined cost of about RMB8.4 million were lost. Adaspace recorded no launch expense because the provider did not meet a contract condition. The quoted fee was about RMB200,000. Adaspace estimates the cost of manufacturing a replacement at RMB2.4 million at its expense, with acceptance of the product and services due by March 2027.

B(ornot)2B analysis

The factual basis appears at the start. We then examine incentives, risks and possible actions. Examples do not describe these companies’ undisclosed contract provisions.

Separate the cost baskets

Our analysis focuses on three separate obligations. The launch service has its own price and performance condition. The payload represents manufacturing expenditure already incurred. The customer contract may require a replacement even when the immediate cause lies with another participant. A remedy concerning one basket can leave the others largely intact.

For a comparable project, trace which costs stay with the provider of the failed service and which stay with the integrator that promised the outcome to its customer. The service fee may be small beside the cost of the lost asset or a second manufacturing cycle. The prospectus describes expense recognition. It does not establish cash movements for the service.

The replacement restarts several clocks

Manufacturing a replacement can consume material, labour and production capacity. It can also require another acceptance process. These are potential consequences to examine, not additional costs quantified for Adaspace. The integrator may need to coordinate the revised delivery date with its customer while resolving responsibility with the launch supplier on a different timetable.

The working-capital question is therefore separate from the liability question. Even a recoverable claim might arrive after money must be committed to fulfil the customer promise. A project review should map the timing of potential reimbursement against the timing of repair or replacement expenditure. A legal entitlement does not automatically fund the next production slot.

Test the interface before pricing the whole project

For a buyer of an integrated service, the useful negotiation is about the boundary between participants. Who supplies another unit? Which acceptance event ends the replacement obligation? Who carries the cost while responsibility is being investigated? Insurance, performance conditions and liability limits need to be read against the same loss scenario.

The disclosed account does not let us judge whether Adaspace chose the wrong launch provider or whether a different arrangement would have been economical. It does give us a sharper way to read a project contract. Trace the physical asset, the service payment and the customer remedy independently, then examine where those obligations reconnect after a failure.
