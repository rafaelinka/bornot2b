The Number

Freight rates rose while shipment volume barely moved

Hapag-Lloyd’s third quarter shows how sharply rates can move on individual trade groups, even when total container volume stays roughly flat.

Hapag-Lloyd · Germany · November 12 · 2 min read

Hardware, documentation and production know-how involve different work. An editorial metaphor.

What was announced

On November 12, Hapag-Lloyd reported an average third-quarter liner freight rate of US$1,808 per TEU, the equivalent of one 20-foot container. That is 28% above the US$1,413 it reported a year earlier. Liner volume fell about 0.5% from 3.401 million to 3.383 million TEU. The company attributes stronger earnings in part to higher spot rates and strong utilization. The disclosed average covers the carrier’s own shipments and can differ from the spot rate or contract quote a buyer sees.

What the average hides

For Asia–Europe, the carrier reported US$1,803 per TEU, up from US$1,275, while the volume fell from 983,000 to 927,000 TEU. For Europe–America, the average went from US$1,538 to US$1,598. The figures cover broad trade groups. The report also says that from Q1 2026 the company counts transport orders after they reach the port of loading, with prior-year figures adjusted. These comparisons use the restated basis.

Bring a lane and a service to the negotiation

A buyer renewing a contract can compare quotes for the same origin, destination, sailing window and equipment type. Break out the base rate and surcharges and ask how a booking is prioritized when ships fill. For example, a lower base rate may lose its advantage if capacity is uncommitted and urgent stock has to be sent another way. The quarterly figures give a reason to check route-level quotes and the service actually received. The carrier’s averages cannot tell a buyer what its lane will cost next month.
