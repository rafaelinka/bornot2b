Deal Economics

How to sell concentrate before a mine starts up

Lindian has signed terms for future shipments of monazite concentrate from Malawi. The public filing shows how a supplier and a buyer can agree on pricing, quality, freight and payment while the plant is still under construction.

Lindian Resources · Australia, Singapore, Malawi · November 10 · 3 min read

Hardware, documentation and production know-how involve different work. An editorial metaphor.

What the companies disclosed

On 10 November, Australia’s Lindian Resources told the ASX that its Singapore subsidiary had signed a binding offtake term sheet with an unnamed refiner. The terms cover 10,000 dry metric tonnes in the first year from January 2027 and 20,000 tonnes a year for five years in Stage 2. Lindian plans its first seaborne shipment for January from the port of Nacala in Mozambique. Deliveries depend on execution of a full offtake agreement and purchase contracts, production at the mine and the required third-party approvals.

What goes into the price per tonne

The parties linked pricing to the Asian Metals Index. The formula accounts for ten rare-earth elements and includes a payability factor. The specification calls for an average total rare-earth oxide content of 54%, with a 52% minimum. The buyer is purchasing concentrate that still needs processing. Comparing two bids for that concentrate therefore requires looking beyond the benchmark. Which elements does each buyer pay for, and what share of their value enters the invoice? Lindian disclosed the pricing mechanism and kept the exact coefficients private.

Who pays for freight and when the bank pays

The term sheet specifies CIF. Under standard CIF terms, the seller pays freight and insurance to the destination port, while the risk of loss or damage transfers to the buyer when the goods are on board at the port of shipment. The discharge port remains to be nominated. A bank letter of credit covering the full invoice is to be issued before shipment. Payment under a documentary credit depends on the seller presenting the required documents. The filing leaves the bank, the document list and the timing of payment undisclosed. For exporters, the practical question is whether the carrier, insurer and inspection parties can produce the documents the credit requires.

What sits behind the 45,000-tonne figure

Lindian’s stated Stage 2 allocation of 45,000 tonnes a year includes 20,000 tonnes under the new terms and up to 25,000 covered by Iluka’s right of first refusal. Iluka already has a separate Stage 1 agreement for about 6,000 tonnes a year. To take up the right to 80% of incremental production from a substantial expansion, Iluka must offer a loan for half its capital cost. The new term sheet makes Stage 2 deliveries subject to regulatory conditions, quota availability and operating performance. The signed terms let Lindian plan for an outlet for part of its future output, while Iluka’s right affects how much the supplier can offer to other buyers. The first shipment, the pricing formula applied to a real cargo and the move to Stage 2 are still ahead.
