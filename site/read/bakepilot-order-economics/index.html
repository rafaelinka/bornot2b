BakePilot: profit starts with the right kilogram

What a bakery needs to know before confirming a wholesale order: units, recipes, discounts, costs, and the boundary between a customer request and a supplier commitment.

Review status: not tested. This is an analysis of public documentation and pricing checked on November 27, 2026. We did not create an account, submit an order, or verify the application's calculations. The numerical example below is our independent hypothetical model.

In manufacturing wholesale, an error can begin well before an invoice. Sales sells a box, production interprets pieces, and the driver receives kilograms. A customer discount looks modest until someone compares it with the cost of that particular batch. Correcting a unit label can therefore matter more than another polished report: it changes what someone physically makes and hands over.

What changed this week

BakePilot's changelog dates the redesigned order form to version 4.9.0 on November 26, linking it to net and gross prices, production costs, and estimated profit. Version 4.9.3 on November 27 corrects quantities for products sold by weight from pieces to kilograms. Branch settings arrived between those releases. These are the developer's release statements; recipes, wholesale price lists, and other modules predate this week.

Our interpretation: assess the update by the continuity of an order. A correct “2.5 kg” in the entry form does not establish that the same unit survives the packing list, delivery note, and export. The object of acceptance is the entire path from the customer promise to physical handover.

The product master comes before the portal

A kilogram of cake, a 250-gram pack, and a carton of six packs are different selling units, even if they contain the same product. Each needs a stable SKU, a link to its recipe and batch yield, a price unit, and an explicit conversion. Otherwise “6” may mean six packs to sales and six cartons to production. Fixing a screen label does not resolve that disagreement.

The wholesale documentation ties costs to a recipe and its yield. Items without usable cost data are flagged and excluded from the overall estimate. The total therefore needs a completeness check: has the whole request been costed, or have some lines dropped out? If one item's cost is unknown, its revenue cannot quietly be treated as additional profit.

The portal is described as a preliminary ordering channel; weight-based products depend on their actual weighed quantity for the final total. Commercially, that means a received request still needs confirmation of quantity, date, and the customer's price. The price-list owner determines the applicable version and discount authority; production confirms what it can supply. Below, a worked control order shows which quantities must reconcile.

Estimated profit and unpaid labor

The calculator has no dedicated hourly labor rate: its FAQ suggests entering labor manually as an amount or through an overhead markup. Meanwhile, the wholesale profit description explicitly says labor is excluded. The public pages do not establish how a particular configuration carries these costs into an order total. Resolve that question with a controlled example before sales starts making decisions from the indicator.

A model can be incomplete without any software defect. Packaging, waste, delivery, energy, and paid hours do not enter it automatically. Comparing the wholesale price with ingredients alone and naming the difference profit is especially dangerous: growing sales can increase utilization while making the economics worse.

A hypothetical batch: the price of one omitted cost

Consider an entirely fictional 20-kilogram batch. Ingredients cost PLN 80, energy PLN 10, packaging PLN 10, labor PLN 40, and other costs PLN 20. That is PLN 160, or PLN 8 per kilogram. This is an independent model, not BakePilot output or a real bakery's data.

Margin here is the difference divided by revenue, rather than a markup on costs. The final row demonstrates deteriorating measurement, not an improving business. Even the first two rows show only a difference after the listed costs, not established accounting profit. The cost boundary must match the decision: can we accept one more order, or does the production operation cover its overall expenses?

A repeat order still contains a decision

The standing-order documentation describes templates generating confirmed orders two days before delivery, with either current or fixed prices. Editing one delivery does not rewrite the template. That is useful for a regular bread route, but an outdated understanding can also keep generating orders.

Consider a fictional customer closing its store on Monday. The template has already created Monday's delivery on Saturday. Three objects now matter: the rule for future orders, the order already created, and the customer agreement. Changing the first does not establish that the other two have been reconciled. The acceptance target is concrete: the unwanted delivery is canceled, production will not make it, the customer receives the agreed notice, and the next required delivery remains intact. This is our proposed scenario, not a test result or a claim about BakePilot's template-deletion behavior.

The cancellation also needs to reconcile with stock. If the order only reserved inventory, release that reservation; if production has finished, decide what happens to the goods. The public description does not establish when a particular BakePilot configuration changes stock. Those transitions need a separate check: a canceled document must not leave unexplained physical goods or inventory already promised to another buyer.

The entry cost exceeds the subscription

On November 27, the official Professional plan costs PLN 189 excluding VAT per month when billed monthly, including five users. Master costs PLN 319 excluding VAT per month when billed monthly and includes the customer portal, standing orders, and additional production processes. Twelve monthly payments total PLN 2,268 and PLN 3,828 respectively. The trial is 20 days without a card. These are Polish service prices, not an assessment of compatibility with another country's tax requirements.

Add recipe preparation, initial stocktaking, customer-master cleanup, and integration maintenance to the budget. For a small bakery, those hours may be the largest expense. Compare the product with a governed alternative: a protected recipe worksheet, one order log, and a daily production summary, each with a clear owner for changes.

The API is described as JSON REST with a limit of 120 requests per minute per key. However, one page assigns access to both Starter and Professional. Confirm API entitlement before purchasing. The existence of an API also does not establish a ready-made connection to your ERP, accounting system, or regulated product-tracking process.

A control order: what must reconcile

Keep the proposed pilot to five fictional products, two buyers, and two price-effective dates. But start with a completed request rather than a tour of screens. The table shows two lines of control order DEMO-014 for Customer A. Its illustrative prices are separate from the earlier cost model and were not produced by the application.

Request lineSelling unit and net priceExpected result
DEMO-W025: 2.5 kg of a weight-based itemKilogram, PLN 60PLN 150; the packing instruction and export retain 2.5 kg
DEMO-P250: six 250-gram packsPack, PLN 12PLN 72; the picker needs six packs with a total weight of 1.5 kg
Two-line totalPrices for the selected buyer and datePLN 222 excluding VAT; total weight 4 kg

This request exposes specific failures. “6 kg” instead of six packs quadruples the physical quantity, even if the interface labels look correct. If the picker receives the right quantities but the export does not, a successful entry screen cannot protect the next stage. After a delivery-date change, the total should follow the agreed price-list version; that acceptance rule must be compared with the service's actual configuration.

The three remaining control products cover other boundaries: a recipe item reconciles with an independent cost calculation, including labor; a manual line without cost leaves the total visibly incomplete; and a six-unit carton has an unambiguous conversion or a recorded limitation. Then cancel the same order and replay its integration message: the repeated message must not create a second delivery. These are prepared acceptance expectations. We have not run them in the application.

The terms provide for a full ZIP export with CSV/XLSX, excluding photos, attachments, and integration secrets. A practical pilot exit is to open that archive and reconstruct DEMO-014: customer, lines, units, prices, and status. Possession of a file does not by itself establish that an operating workflow can move to another system.

BakePilot merits a pilot where wholesale requests feed in-house production. A pure reseller with a working ERP may have little use for its recipe depth. The useful output is a record of confirmed order transitions and known limitations. That record can be weighed against data-preparation work and the subscription price.
