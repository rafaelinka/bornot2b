BakePilot: profit starts with the right kilogram

What a bakery needs to know before confirming a wholesale order: units, recipes, discounts, costs, and the boundary between a customer request and a supplier commitment.

Review status: not tested. This is an analysis of public documentation and pricing checked on November 27, 2026. We did not create an account, submit an order, or verify the application's calculations. The numerical example below is our independent hypothetical model.

In manufacturing wholesale, an error can begin well before an invoice. Sales sells a box, production interprets pieces, and the driver receives kilograms. A customer discount looks modest until someone compares it with the cost of that particular batch. Correcting a unit label can therefore matter more than another polished report: it changes what someone physically makes and hands over.

What changed this week

BakePilot's changelog dates the redesigned order form to version 4.9.0 on November 26, linking it to net and gross prices, production costs, and estimated profit. Version 4.9.3 on November 27 corrects quantities for products sold by weight from pieces to kilograms. Branch settings arrived between those releases. These are the developer's release statements; recipes, wholesale price lists, and other modules predate this week.

Our interpretation: assess the update by the continuity of an order. A correct “2.5 kg” in the entry form does not establish that the same unit survives the packing list, delivery note, and export. The object of acceptance is the entire path from the customer promise to physical handover.

The product master comes before the portal

The minimum foundation is a stable SKU, a selling unit, recipe yield, and someone responsible for the price. Define a kilogram of cake, a 250-gram pack, and a carton of six packs separately. They are different commercial objects even when they contain the same product. An ambiguous pack conversion can turn automation into a faster way of replicating a mistake.

The wholesale documentation ties costs to a recipe and its yield. Items without usable cost data are excluded from the overall estimate and flagged separately. A green total therefore needs another question: how much of the order has actually been costed? An unknown cost should trigger review, rather than imply a free product.

The next layer is the customer's terms: which price list applies to the delivery date, and who may grant an additional discount? A sensible launch starts with manually reconciling several orders against an independent worksheet. Only then open self-service. A customer interface reduces rekeying; it cannot supply a missing pricing control.

The portal is described as a preliminary ordering channel; weight-based products depend on their actual weighed quantity for the final total. The practical implication is a supplier-owned confirmation procedure for quantity, price, and delivery date. A received request and an accepted commitment should be distinguishable to both the employee and the buyer.

Estimated profit and unpaid labor

The calculator has no dedicated hourly labor rate: its FAQ suggests entering labor manually as an amount or through an overhead markup. Meanwhile, the wholesale profit description explicitly says labor is excluded. The public pages do not establish how a particular configuration carries these costs into an order total. Resolve that question with a controlled example before sales starts making decisions from the indicator.

A model can be incomplete without any software defect. Packaging, waste, delivery, energy, and paid hours do not enter it automatically. Comparing the wholesale price with ingredients alone and naming the difference profit is especially dangerous: growing sales can increase utilization while making the economics worse.

A hypothetical batch: the price of one omitted cost

Consider an entirely fictional 20-kilogram batch. Ingredients cost PLN 80, energy PLN 10, packaging PLN 10, labor PLN 40, and other costs PLN 20. That is PLN 160, or PLN 8 per kilogram. This is an independent model, not BakePilot output or a real bakery's data.

Margin here is the difference divided by revenue, rather than a markup on costs. The final row demonstrates deteriorating measurement, not an improving business. Even the first two rows show only a difference after the listed costs, not established accounting profit. The cost boundary must match the decision: can we accept one more order, or does the production operation cover its overall expenses?

A repeat order still contains a decision

The standing-order documentation describes templates generating confirmed orders two days before delivery, with either current or fixed prices. Editing one delivery does not rewrite the template. That is useful for a regular bread route, but also creates a point where an outdated understanding becomes a repeated daily action.

Who pauses generation for a holiday? How does the buyer learn about a price change? Who checks orders already created after a template is deleted? Those rules belong to the commercial process. An application status alone does not establish customer agreement to revised terms.

Inventory also needs a check of timing. Agree what an order, a completed batch, and customer handover each mean, then examine cancellation at every stage. Otherwise stock can appear available after being promised, or disappear before production has happened. We have not tested those transitions in the application.

The entry cost exceeds the subscription

On November 27, the official Professional plan costs PLN 189 excluding VAT per month when billed monthly, including five users. Master costs PLN 319 excluding VAT per month when billed monthly and includes the customer portal, standing orders, and additional production processes. Twelve monthly payments total PLN 2,268 and PLN 3,828 respectively. The trial is 20 days without a card. These are Polish service prices, not an assessment of compatibility with another country's tax requirements.

Add recipe preparation, initial stocktaking, customer-master cleanup, and integration maintenance to the budget. For a small bakery, those hours may be the largest expense. Compare the product with a governed alternative: a protected recipe worksheet, one order log, and a daily production summary, each with a clear owner for changes.

The API is described as JSON REST with a limit of 120 requests per minute per key. However, one page assigns access to both Starter and Professional. Confirm API entitlement before purchasing. The existence of an API also does not establish a ready-made connection to your ERP, accounting system, or regulated product-tracking process.

How to evaluate it without a live customer order

Our proposed pilot uses five fictional products, two buyers, and two price-effective dates. The production owner can block rollout over any ambiguous unit. Run it on the intended plan and through the actual documents the shift will use.

  • A weight-based item: 2.5 kg at PLN 60 totals PLN 150; kilograms survive through export.
  • Six 250-gram packs: PLN 72 at PLN 12 per pack; physical weight remains 1.5 kg.
  • A recipe-based item: reconcile cost against an independent calculation, checking labor separately.
  • A manual line without cost: incompleteness remains visible; a complete profit estimate is not implied.
  • A carton of six units: the conversion is either explicitly supported or recorded as a limitation.

Next, change the date, cancel a delivery, repeat an integration event, and inspect stock. Replaying one message must not create a second physical delivery. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not tests we performed.

Finally, examine the exit. The terms provide for a full ZIP export with CSV/XLSX, excluding photos, attachments, and integration secrets. Obtaining an archive and restoring an operating workflow elsewhere are separate tasks. BakePilot merits a pilot where wholesale orders feed in-house production. A pure reseller with a working ERP may have little use for its recipe depth. A purchase should survive scrutiny of units, cost completeness, and the ability to leave.
