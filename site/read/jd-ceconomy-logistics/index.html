Routes to Market

JD.com bought control of CECONOMY. What can other retailers now access?

A takeover gives JD.com a retail network. A separate commitment gives rival retailers access to its logistics on the same terms as CECONOMY.

JD.com × CECONOMY · China, Germany · November 12 · 3 min read

Hardware, documentation and production know-how involve different work. An editorial metaphor.

What the companies disclosed

China’s JD.com closed its takeover of Germany’s CECONOMY on November 12. It paid €1.335 billion at €4.60 a share for 59.8% of the share capital and voting rights and began consolidating CECONOMY that day. The founding family’s investment vehicle, Convergenta, kept a separate 25.3% stake. The deal brings more than 1,000 MediaMarkt, Saturn and MediaWorld stores across 11 markets into JD.com’s group, alongside CECONOMY’s online channels.

What JD.com agreed to share

The European Commission cleared the transaction on November 3, subject to commitments. Its investigation found that JD.com had received preferential financing, tax incentives and grants from China and that those subsidies could distort competition. The commitments last ten years and will be monitored by an independent trustee. Intragroup transactions between JD.com and CECONOMY, including financing, must take place on market terms. JD.com must offer its warehousing and logistics services to third-party retailers on the same terms it gives CECONOMY, with a similar commitment covering its technology services for third parties.

Consider what that means to another retailer. If CECONOMY receives a JD.com service on a set of terms, a qualifying rival can seek those same terms for that service. The commercial value will be clearer when retailers have an actual quote to compare, with prices, included operations and lead times. Faster deliveries remain an ambition, and post-close performance is still to be measured.

What suppliers should check

A single retail brand can already play different roles on the same website. In Germany, MMS E-Commerce GmbH operates MediaMarkt’s own web shop. On the German marketplace, the third-party merchant sells in its own name, contracts directly with the customer and is responsible for fulfilment. In a wholesale arrangement to stock the retailer’s own assortment, the buying entity named in the supply agreement is the one to look to for purchase orders and payment. Warehousing and delivery can sit under a separate service agreement in either route.

The economics depend on who orders and pays for the stock, who holds it, and who bears the cost of returns. Those roles can differ even if the same warehouse handles both boxes. For each market, a supplier can verify the buying entity, any commitment to purchase stock, responsibility for returns, and the delivery promise. CECONOMY says it will continue to operate as a stand-alone European business with a local, independent technology stack. The companies have yet to disclose any resulting changes to supplier terms, assortment or order routing.
