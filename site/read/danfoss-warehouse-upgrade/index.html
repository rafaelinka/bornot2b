How do you accept an upgrade at a working distribution center?

Danfoss is replacing warehouse systems dating to 2001. A phased rollout gives the buyer several chances to test whether a new system can take over.

Danfoss × Toyota Automated Logistics · Denmark / Japan · November 12 · 2 min read

Hardware, documentation and production know-how involve different work. An editorial metaphor.

What was announced

On November 12, Toyota Automated Logistics said Danfoss had commissioned it as the general contractor for an upgrade at its central distribution center in Rødekro, Denmark. The scope includes replacing pallet conveyors with autonomous mobile robots that bring pallets to workers, and adding robotic systems for small parts. Danfoss logistics executive Dan Bruun said installation was expected to start in February 2027, with go-live planned for December 2028. The announcement gives no contract value, expected throughput or downtime allowance.

B(ornot)2B analysis

Define success at each handoff

A migration test can start with one flow, such as moving picked pallets to dispatch. The buyer and integrator would agree which location and status the warehouse system should record at handoff, how a stalled pallet returns to the queue and how teams reconcile the stock at the end of a shift. A realistic test batch includes awkward items, replenishment and exceptions, because a clean demonstration under easy conditions tells the buyer little about a difficult shift.

Keep the option to pause

For a phased retrofit, ask who can stop the next cutover, what would trigger a rollback and how the old route would resume. Agree what counts as acceptance for each phase, including order accuracy and sustained throughput during the buyer’s actual order mix. The seller gains a clear scope to price and demonstrate. The buyer gets evidence before moving the next flow. These are procurement checks suggested by the project structure. The parties have not disclosed how the Danfoss acceptance tests or fallback will work.
